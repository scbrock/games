What to notice next time

A routine for every move

Strong players don't see more by magic. They look at the same things in the same order, every time. Use this routine on every puzzle and in your own games until it's automatic. The coach on the Train tab walks you through it.

  1. What did their move change?Every move does two things: it attacks something new, and it stops guarding something. Ask both. Most blunders, theirs and yours, live here.
  2. Checks, captures, threatsList every check, then every capture, then every move that threatens something. Forcing moves limit your opponent's choices, so they're the easiest to calculate and the most likely to win.
  3. Loose pieces and the king"Loose pieces drop off." Spot every undefended piece, theirs and yours. Count the enemy king's free squares. Few squares means a mating idea is likely.
  4. Candidates, then calculateChoose two or three candidate moves. For each, find their most forcing reply, then your answer. Stop when the position is quiet and count the material.
  5. Blunder check, then moveBefore you touch the piece: after this move, what are their checks and captures? If nothing tactical works, improve your worst piece instead.

Calculating well

  • Forcing first. Calculate checks and captures before quiet moves. They have the fewest replies.
  • Assume the best defence. Don't hope. Ask what you'd play in their shoes.
  • Finish the line. Keep going until there are no more checks or captures, then evaluate.
  • Look for the in-between move. Before recapturing automatically, check whether there's a stronger move first.
  • Try to refute your favourite. Look hard for the reply that beats the move you like, before you play it.
